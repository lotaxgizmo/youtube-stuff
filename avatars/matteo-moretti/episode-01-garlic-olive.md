There is a garlic clove in your kitchen right now, and there is a bottle of olive oil in the cabinet beside it. You walked right by both of them this morning. And the two of them together are the oldest morning ritual in my part of the world, and in the whole Mediterranean, and I want to show it to you before you reach for the bottle with the label and the sixty dollars on it. I am Matteo Moretti. I grow olives in the hills of Tuscany, near Montepulciano, and my family has pressed oil from these terraces for four generations. And the thing I am about to show you, I learned standing at my nonna Rosina's table, watching her peel a single clove of garlic before the day started. Let me say this the way I will always say it, so there is no confusion. I am not a doctor. I have never claimed to be one, and I never will be. What I am is an olive man whose grandfather took a clove of garlic and a spoonful of his own oil every single morning of his long life. This is educational, and it is not medical advice. Now, let me be straight with you before we go a step further, because I would rather you trust me than like me. This is not a cure. There is no cure in a spoonful of anything, and if I told you one clove of garlic will fix your stomach in three days, you should stop watching right now, because that is not how the world works, and I will not sell you a lie to get a view. What I can tell you is what has been passed down on my side of the water for as long as anyone remembers. My grandfather, Girolamo, took his garlic and oil every morning of his life, and he worked in those groves well into his nineties, and when people asked him why his stomach never troubled him, he would hold up the clove and say, "fresca la mattina" — fresh in the morning — and that was the whole answer he gave. Now, I am not going to stand here and tell you the garlic did that. Old age is not a receipt, and plenty of men who never touched garlic live just as long. What I am telling you is tradition, honestly: what my people did, why they did it, and how to do it so that at best you are supporting your own gut in a gentle way, and at worst you have spent nine cents and a minute of your morning. There is no bottle on the shelf that can promise you better than that.

Here is why the old way beats the new way, and nobody bothers to explain this part. Walk into any store and there is a shelf of digestive things, and they are mostly the same three ingredients dressed up in expensive boxes, with a list of chemicals you cannot pronounce and a price tag that says they know something you do not. My nonna's shelf had none of it. It had a braid of garlic hanging by the window, a stone jar of oil from our own press, salt, lemons, and a jug of water from the well. And here is the thing the store will never tell you: the raw materials are the same ones their expensive bottle started as, except theirs has been processed, extracted, concentrated, and put in a capsule so you can pay more for less. Garlic is garlic. Oil is oil. My grandfather's morning meal was the original version, and the original version costs nothing and comes with a story, and the story is half of what makes it work, whether the medicine comes from the pharmacy or the meaning comes from the table. A cold press keeps everything the tree gave the olive. That is why we press cold and slow at the frantoio, and that is why my nonna never boiled anything if she could help it. Gentle and slow is not a compromise. Gentle and slow is the whole method.

So let me tell you exactly what this is, and I will be specific, because specifics are the whole difference between a family practice and a mistake. One clove of raw garlic, fresh. One teaspoon of good olive oil, cold-pressed, the kind that comes out green-gold and tastes like the tree, not the kind in the plastic bottle that could be anything. A small pinch of sea salt. And warm water to follow. That is the entire list, and it will cost you less than the drive to the store, because you almost certainly own all of it already. And before the method, the tell, because the tell is what everyone leaves out. The garlic should be firm, heavy for its size, with papery skin that is not yellow, and no green sprout starting in the middle. A clove that has sprouted has already spent its strength on the shoot, and it will be hotter and harder on your stomach. The oil should look like fresh grass in the bottle, not pale and flat. If it is pale and flat, it is not the oil my grandfather was drinking, and you are wasting your nine cents. And the salt, a pinch, the way you would for bread. That is the tell. Three small things, and every one of them is checkable in your own kitchen before you swallow anything.

Here is the exact method, step by step, the way I watched it done since I was a boy, and the way I do it to this day. First, peel your clove and crush it, or chop it fine. Do not swallow it whole — the whole point is to break the clove open first, and let it sit for a minute on the board. Even my grandfather, who ate garlic like an apple, chopped it before he swallowed it, because a crushed clove is a different thing than a whole one, and the minute it sits is the minute the strong parts wake up. Second, warm nothing. We keep it cool: the crushed garlic into a small spoon with the olive oil, the pinch of salt, and if the taste is too much, stir it into a little lukewarm water and drink it like that. Third, and this is the order everybody gets wrong, take it first thing, on an empty stomach, before coffee, before bread. Then drink a glass of warm water on top of it, and wait a short while before you eat. That wait is not decoration. It is the whole practice. You are giving the oil and the garlic a clear road, nothing else ahead of them, and the warm water gives them a gentle start. And fourth, here is the part that makes it a habit instead of a stunt: once a day, every day, or not at all. One clove is enough. Two is a mistake. Three is a mistake that will teach you not to do it. My grandfather did not measure it out stingily, but he did it the same way every morning, and the sameness is what he trusted. The tree does not get bigger because you water it twice in one day. You water it every year, and you let the years do the work. That is the method. It is simple, and simple is not the same as worthless.

Let me be straight with you, the way I would be across a table. First, and I cannot say this enough, a good press starts with a good olive. That is what I tell every man who brings his own olives to the press, and it is what I am telling you now. Know your own body before you put anything in it. If your stomach is easily turned, or you have acid reflux, or a sensitive gut that you already argue with every day, raw garlic on an empty stomach is not the thing for you, and starting with it is how you end up hating a practice your grandmother's grandmother swore by. If you take blood thinners, or any medicine that thins the blood, garlic has a way of talking to the same machinery, so you check with your doctor first, and you are not going to like me saying that, and I am going to say it anyway.
