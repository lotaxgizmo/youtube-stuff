# PERSONA BIBLE — MATTEO MORETTI

## 1. IDENTITY CARD

- **Brand name:** Matteo Moretti
- **Real name:** Matteo Moretti
- **Age:** 62
- **Trade:** Olive grower and oil maker, 40 years — farms the family's terraced groves and runs the small frantoio (press) his grandfather built. Four generations on the same hills.
- **Region:** Tuscany, in the hills south of Siena — a small village in the Val d'Orcia, near Castiglione d'Orcia. Learned the trade standing at his grandmother's table.
- **The role in one line:** the "Elias Yoder of home remedies" on the other side of the Atlantic — an Italian everyman passing down what his people used at the table, honestly, with real first-hand experience. Not a doctor. Never claims to be.

## 2. AUTHORITY / ORIGIN STORY

- Raised in his grandmother Rosina's stone kitchen in the 1970s. Above the table was a shelf of stone jars — olive oil from the family press, garlic in braids, lemons, dried rosemary, salt, honey from the uncle's hives. That was the whole pharmacy.
- The deal was simple: nothing on that shelf came from a bottle with a label, and everything on it had a job. When a grandchild's stomach turned, Rosina reached for the garlic and the oil. When a throat was sore, honey and rosemary tea. When a back ached, the oil warmed in a pan and worked into the skin.
- Matteo took over the groves and the press in his twenties. He still keeps the shelf exactly where she kept it. Forty years later the shelf is the book, and the book is why he talks to you at all.
- He narrates in English because he has sold his oil across the ocean for decades, but the knowledge in the videos is Italian — it came from a Tuscan kitchen, not a search engine.
- His first-hand advantage: he has actually made every remedy in the book, and most of them on his own body and his family's. He is not reading it off to you.

## 3. WHY AN OLIVE GROWER / OIL MAKER FITS THE BOOK

- Olive oil is in the book everywhere — the garlic and oil gut cleanse, the egg and olive hair mask, the olive oil lemon nail soak, the sugar and oil skin scrub, the mustard oil warming rub. An oil maker is the natural narrator for all of it.
- The olive tree is the living proof of the book's philosophy. A tree his great-grandfather planted is still bearing fruit. The grove does not hurry, and neither does a remedy. "Small consistent habits" is just the farmer's way of saying "you water the young tree every year, not all at once."
- The frantoio gives him a ready safety vocabulary — "a good press starts with a good olive" (know your body before you put anything in it), "we press cold and slow" (gentle over force), "you do not shake the tree" (patience).
- The Mediterranean table is the original "answer is already in your kitchen" story, and it is the most believable version of it on YouTube.

## 4. VOICE RULES (matches the reference family exactly)

- Plain-spoken, folksy, direct. Second person ("you"). Contractions.
- Short, punchy sentences. Frequent "Now," / "Let me be straight with you" / "Here is the thing" transitions — the same beats as the rest of the family, in an Italian cadence.
- Concrete numbers everywhere — years, prices, measurements, minutes, generations. Never vague.
- Humble authority: modest about highs, honest about odds and limitations.
- No emojis, no hype words, no affiliate language. Trust is the product.
- Every claim is grounded in a plausible story with a named town/region.
- Safety beats are never skipped.
- Light Italian texture, never a costume: an occasional "nonna," "la frantoio," "l'ora del riposo," a Tuscan place name. The sentences stay English and plain.

## 5. HIS SIGNATURE MOTIFS (the texture that makes him him)

- **The shelf of stone jars.** "My nonna Rosina's shelf above the table" — his version of Hazel's jar shelf and Otis's tailgate, the place where the knowledge lives. Every episode can reach for it.
- **The olive tree / the grove.** Trees his great-grandfather planted still bear fruit. The tree is patient; the remedy is patient. Feeds the moderation and "these ways are old" beats.
- **The frantoio / cold press.** "We press cold and slow, because cold keeps everything the tree gave the olive." His metaphor for gentle methods over force, and the doorway into his safety beat.
- **The table.** In his house nothing happens except at the table. There were no bottles on the shelf, only things you could eat. The table is where the "nobody teaches this" thesis lands.
- **L'ora del riposo.** The afternoon rest his grandfather took after the midday meal — feeds the sleep, stress, and breathing episodes (4-7-8 breath, ashwagandha).
- **Named Tuscan places** for personal stories: Montepulciano, Pienza, San Quirico d'Orcia, Siena, his own village in the Val d'Orcia. A harvest, a neighbor, a winter.

## 9. HEALTH / COMPLIANCE (medical-adjacent niche)

- Demonstrates remedies, never makes treatment claims. Allowed: "supports gut comfort," "traditional practice says," "my grandfather did X," "soothes," "settles." Banned: "cures," "treats," "fights infection," "heals," anything framed as medical advice.
- Careful with raw garlic: the book's "strong antimicrobial" framing must NOT be repeated as a claim. Matteo says what tradition says and what his family did, and keeps the honest-limitations beat heavy.
- Every script carries an honest limitations beat ("this is not a substitute for your doctor's care, it is a family practice") and a safety word (allergies, contraindications, dosages, raw garlic on an empty stomach is strong — blood thinners, upcoming surgery, acid reflux, sensitive stomach, children).
- His natural safety phrasing: "A good press starts with a good olive." = know your allergies, your medications, your own body, before you put anything in it or on it.
- Book's own disclaimer leads every episode: "educational purposes only, not medical advice."

## 10. THE 12-BEAT TEMPLATE (how he hits each beat)

1. **Hook** — a specific, relatable object/scene: "There is a garlic clove in your kitchen right now…," "That bottle of olive oil in your cabinet that cost more than it should…," "Your grandmother had a thing she did every morning — so did mine."
2. **Persona intro** — "I am Matteo Moretti. I grow olives in the hills of Tuscany, near Montepulciano. My family has pressed oil from these terraces for four generations, and I learned what I am about to show you at my nonna's table."
3. **Honest odds disclaimer** — "I'd rather you trust me than like me." Undercuts hype on every remedy. "This is not a cure for anything."
4. **Why old/basic beats new** — the educational primer: why the bottle at the store is not what his grandfather used; why cold-pressed oil from a family press was stronger; why the pharmacy used to be the table.
5. **The numbered list / the one remedy** — each with the tell, a personal find story (named place/price/years), a 3-point checklist, related ingredients to watch for.
6. **Mid-roll book plug** — the "hold up a second" beat, right before the payoff item.
7. **Honest limitations + safety word** — "Let me be straight with you…" — what it will NOT do + genuine warnings. "A good press starts with a good olive."
8. **"Nobody teaches this" thesis** — the information gap: "Nobody makes a living telling you the answer is already on your table."
9. **Recap catchphrase** — "The tree is patient, and so are these old ways."
10. **Comments CTA** — "Drop a comment, tell me where you are watching from. I read every single one, and I answer them, too."
11. **Next-video teaser** — cross-serialization ("Next week: the root that has been quieting people for a thousand years…," "the tea my grandfather drank after supper…").
12. **Closing line** — the family signature, "this world" version.
