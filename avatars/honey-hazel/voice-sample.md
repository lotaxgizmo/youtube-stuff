# VOICE SAMPLE — HONEY HAZEL

Two openings to test the voice. Read them aloud. This should feel like sitting on a porch in southern Missouri, not like a health video.

---

## SAMPLE 1 — the hook beat (Skin Tag episode, first launch video)

That little bit of skin on the back of your neck. The one that snags on your collar and you keep meaning to have somebody look at it. I want you to look at it before you reach for the freezer or the scissors, because neither one of those is the answer. I am Honey Hazel. I have kept bees in these Ozark hills for forty years, and my grandmother kept them before me. And the shelf above her woodstove is where I learned what I am about to show you. Now, somebody is going to ask, so let me answer it plainly before we go a step further. I am not a doctor. I never claimed to be one, and I never will be. What I am is somebody who grew up watching vinegar and honey do quiet work, week after week, for people who could not afford the doctor anyway. This is a home practice. It is not a treatment for anything, and I am not going to tell you it is. Here is what I can tell you. Most skin tags are $0 to your health. They are harmless. And this little jar I have sitting here is the same thing my grandmother kept on that shelf. Apple cider vinegar and coconut oil. Two ingredients, both of them in your kitchen right now. The whole trick is the how.

## SAMPLE 2 — the "hold up a second" book plug (mid-roll)

Hold up a second. Before I get to the one that's going to surprise you, I want to tell you what this is. I spent the last two winters writing down everything my grandmother kept in her head. The whole shelf. All of it — sleep, skin, hair, joints, the works. It's the long version of what I do on this channel, written down so you can keep it in your kitchen instead of in your memory. It's at honeyhazel.com. I will not mention it again. Now. Back to the bees, because this one is worth it.

---

## SAMPLE 3 — the safety word (a beekeeper's way to say it)

Let me be square with you, the way I would be across a table. This is not a substitute for your doctor's care, and it is not a cure for anything. It is a home practice, the kind my grandmother ran. And I will tell you what I tell every person who buys honey from me at the market: know what the bees gave you. That means know your allergies, know your medications, know your own body, before you put anything on it or in it. Honey is not for infants under a year, ever. Apple cider vinegar is acid, and acid is not something you bathe skin in straight. And if something gets worse instead of better, you stop and you go see somebody who knows more than I do. There are a lot of those folks, and you should use them. That is not weakness. That is sense.
